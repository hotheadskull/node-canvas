Observatory — freeform no grid, no columns · 14 nodes placed by hand

The columns in the earlier canvases were my layout, not the design's requirement. Lanes were never meant to be a grid you place nodes into — they are found in the empty space you leave behind. Drop nodes wherever you think, and the router reads the gaps between them as corridors, then runs wires down those corridors in lanes. Move a node and the corridors are recomputed. The rigid look survives because the wires are rigid, not the placement.

How freeform and rigid coexist placement is yours · routing is the program's
1 · corridors are found
Every node's box is inflated by a margin; what's left over is free space. The vertical and horizontal channels through it are the corridors. Nobody draws them — they fall out of where you put things.
2 · lanes fill the corridor
Inside a corridor, wires take lanes 14px apart in target order. Same chamfers, same hops, same ties as before — the grammar never changed, only where it lives.
3 · ports on all four sides
Left and right stay the default take and give. When a partner sits above or below, the port moves to that edge instead — so a wire never has to loop around a plate to reach it.
4 · what you give up
Moving one node can re-route another's wire. Beziers never do that. It's the price of never tracing spaghetti — and the ghost-then-settle on drop is what makes it legible rather than jumpy.
Very tight clusters lose their corridors. Under about 40px of gap there's nowhere to route, so wires stub straight across. The canvas nudges spacing on drop rather than forbidding the placement.
Snap 24 and Tidy in the toolbar are opt-in. Free placement is the default — the grid is a convenience, never a requirement.

The dense canvas and this one are the same system at two densities: corridors here, trunks and highways there. Columns were only ever the tidiest arrangement, not the required one.
