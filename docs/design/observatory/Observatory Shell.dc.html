What changed, and why four rules — each one is a small diff in the code
1 · Ports keep their colour
wired — filled, glowing
open — outlined, same hue
wanted — outlined + amber pip
Before, an unused port went grey and you lost what it accepted. Now the hue never leaves — fill means wired, outline means open, and the amber pip is the old flagWhenEmpty. You can read what a plate accepts from across the canvas.
2 · A group is a node
Cast104
door-open = enter · package-open = dissolve
Not a dashed region — a real plate with a type spine, ports and a readiness ring, distinguished only by the layers mark and a density strip instead of prose. It wires like anything else, so a hundred people occupy one plate's worth of canvas.
3 · Blocks live on the plate
01from a nodelive
02edited hereforked
03typed hereown
04type, or drop a node…
The block list is on the Document plate, not hidden in a room. Three left-border colours carry the whole embed story, and the last row is always an empty one you can type straight into.
4 · Rooms are the dock
Canvas — relationships
Document — block order
Arc — argument order
Focus — no order, just prose
Four icons, always in the same place, always showing whatever you last selected. The canvas is the only view with wires; each room is one ordering of the same data.
Assemble and take apart two gestures each way, and both are reversible
Gather⌘G
Select any nodes and gather them. Wires that crossed the boundary re-attach to the new plate's ports; wires inside come along untouched.
Enterdbl-click
The group opens as its own canvas. The outside world becomes edge stubs down the sides, so you can work on the 104 without the other 64 nodes in the way.
Dissolve⌘⇧G
The plate goes away and the members land loose on the canvas, keeping every wire. Nothing is deleted and nothing is merged — dissolve is the exact inverse of gather.
Split⌘⌥S
Pick a count and a type; the children appear already wired back to the parent's spine, splitting from the junction dot. Split a child the same way — depth is unlimited.

Scale rule behind all of it: a group costs one plate no matter how many members it holds. Fifty sources and a hundred characters are two plates and two wires on this canvas — the density strips say how much is inside and how much of it is done, and you enter a group only when you want to work in it.
