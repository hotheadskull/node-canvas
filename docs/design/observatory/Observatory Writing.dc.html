Observatory — where the writing goes the plate has two jobs and they want opposite things

You're right, and it's structural rather than cosmetic. Everything built so far is a map — the plate exists to be scanned, so registry label, id, status, counts and ports earn their space and the prose gets a two-line excerpt. But a scene is two thousand words, a sermon point is a paragraph and a passage, a paper section is eight hundred words of argued prose. None of that fits, and it shouldn't: a map that showed full text would stop being a map. The fix is that the plate has a second state where the ratio inverts — the chrome recedes to almost nothing and the text gets real typographic treatment. Same object, same position on the canvas, opposite priorities.

The open question underneath this: whether long-form writing happens on the canvas like this, or whether the canvas stays a map and a separate editor view owns the prose. Opening in place keeps one mental model and one place things live; a split view gives the text a permanent home and a wider column. I've drawn the in-place version because it protects the thing that makes this app different — but it's a fork worth deciding deliberately.
