You were right and I was wrong: a character is not group material. A group is for things you want to treat as one — a character is the opposite, a hub whose whole value is its individual connections. The registry already knows who is in what (Section takes people · person · ∞, Person gives person). The problem was purely visual, and the fix is one rule: membership is a mark on the plate; content flow is a wire. Twelve characters across twenty-four chapters is 180 relationships — as wires it is a hairball, as marks it is three small strips you can read at a glance.

Content flow → a wire
The note's text goes into Yusuf. Chapter 12's prose goes into the manuscript. Something actually moves, and you need to see where — so it gets a routed, animated strand.
A character is a hub, not group material: notes, wants, possessions and questions all hang off it as real wires. Only the appears-in relationships become marks.
Select to promote
Marks are the resting state. Select a person and their memberships become wires for as long as they're selected — chapters they're in stay lit, the rest drop to 42%. Deselect and the canvas goes quiet again. One character at a time is four wires; all twelve at once is never drawn.
