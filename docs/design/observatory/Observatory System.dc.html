Observatory — the system 16 node types · 11 data kinds · 4 readiness stages · 5 split presets

Built against core/src/registry.ts, derive.ts and blocks.ts rather than invented. The single change that makes the whole thing cohere: colour comes from the port's data kind, not the node's type. A wire is the colour of what travels down it, and a plate's spine is the colour of what it gives. Sixteen node types collapse to eleven learnable hues, and every one of them is already a value in your registry.

Relation words — supports, rebuts, serves — ride the wire as a label. They no longer need their own hue.

This replaces the eleven invented relation hues from earlier. A dataKind: 'any' port takes the colour of whatever plugs into it, which is why Claim's supports-in shows green from a Claim and blue from a Source.

2 · Readiness derive.ts — seed · developing · ready · placed
seed
a title and nothing else
developing
text exists, wires are incomplete
ready
complete, and nothing is flagged
placed
wired into a document that compiles it
A ring, not a coloured dot — so readiness never competes with the data-kind hues, and stays legible for anyone who can't separate amber from green. Only placed gets a fill, because it's the only stage that means the work has found its home.

5 · Blocks & embeds blocks.ts — this is how the master node gets built from the small ones
A document is a stack of blocks
own text Ghazan's seal opened every gate between Tabriz and the Wall.
embed§ Kaffa The gates closed from the inside, which was the part nobody could explain afterwards. live
forked§ Kaffa The gates closed from the inside — and nobody could explain it afterwards, least of all Yusuf.Revert to sourceApply to source
new Write here, or drop a node to embed it…
Embeds are the composition mechanism. Wire a Section into a Document and its text appears as a block — live, still owned by the source. The moment you edit it here it forks: the document keeps its own version and the source is untouched, exactly as blocks.ts has it. Write-back is a deliberate act, never a side effect of typing.
Fork notice, on the source

The source says when a document has diverged from it. Without this, forking silently is a trap — you'd edit the source and wonder why the manuscript never changed.
Both directions
Upward. Write fragments, wire them into a Document, reorder the intake. The master node is the last thing that exists.
Downward. Start with one node, split it by preset into stubs already wired back to it, then fill them in.
Both at once. Nothing distinguishes a node made by splitting from one made by hand, so you can switch direction mid-thought.

8 · Port grammar every attribute in the registry has one visual consequence
direction
give — right edge
take — left edge
Side carries direction, so a wire never needs an arrowhead.
capacity
one — a single slot
many — a stacked slot
A doubled slot accepts more. Full single slots refuse the drop.
spine
010203
The ordered intake. Numbered, draggable — this is the outline.
defaultVisible
shown
hidden until used
Rare ports stay out of sight. Wire one and it appears for good.
flagWhenEmpty ⚑
unfilled, and it matters
The port asks for something. It's the difference between an empty slot and a missing one — and it's what holds a node at developing.
9 · Rooms four views on one document — the toolbar tabs now mean something
Canvas
The map. Everything at once, spatially. Where you think.
Document room
The block stack on the left, the compiled prose on the right. Reorder here and the spine port reorders with it.
Arc room
Propositions in order as a shape. For a sermon or an argument, where the sequence is the work.
Focus editor
One column, no canvas, no rail. For the hour when you're not navigating, just writing.
