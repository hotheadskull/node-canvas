Observatory — fan-in, depth and the Hub three answers

Short versions: yes, a port takes as many wires as it gives — capacity: 'many' is already in your registry and it works in both directions. The map plate isn't where lore lives — it's a label; the open state is the room. And yes, you're missing a type — what you're describing isn't a Group, and it isn't a Document. It's a third thing.

1 · One port, many wires capacity is symmetric — a many-port fans in exactly as it fans out
Up to four — separate lanes
A many port is a stack of slots, one per wire, with one open slot always waiting at the bottom. Each note keeps its own strand and its own landing, so you can trace any of them back.
More than four — one tied bundle
Past four, the strands tie into one band with a count — the same trunk language the dense canvas uses. Hover the tie and they separate; the slot itself thickens so you can see it's carrying a lot.

3 · The Hub — a type you're missing not a Group, not a Document

A Group contains — members go inside and off the canvas. A Document composes — members' text flows in and comes out as prose. What you're describing does neither: the characters and items of a city stay right where they are, fully visible, still wired to everything else. They just also belong to Kaffa. That's a third verb — a Hub collects.

Three ways a node can hold others pick by what you want to happen to the members
Group contains
Members leave the canvas. Wires that crossed the boundary re-attach to the face. For inventory you don't want to look at.
Hub collects
Members stay exactly where they are, fully wired to everything else. The hub only lists them. For a subject with many facets.
Document composes
Members' text flows in, in order, and comes out as one manuscript. For the thing you actually publish.
